Milan: waiting in the sun

How many minutes do riders wait in direct sun at Milan's bus and tram stops? For each stop the map combines a sun-exposure score (0 in building shadow, under a tree crown, in sun with a shelter, 1 in full sun, no shelter) with the estimated wait, over the hottest documented hours of Milan's hottest summer since 1951 — June 28, 2026, 13:00–19:00 CEST (12:00–18:00 in the ARPA solar-time data). The metric is sun-exposed waiting minutes, not a heat-stress index: air temperature and humidity are not modelled.

    Method. Waits use a mixed model: random arrivals plus a share of riders synchronised to the timetable (a parameter, up to for headways of min or more), so each stop has a central estimate and a low–high range. The shelter factor () is an assumption, not calibrated: changing it between and moves a large share of stops to another exposure class, which the dashed rings flag. Trees come from the Comune di Milano census (ds2484, 31 March 2025) and include municipal trees only: private trees, and trees in parks or courtyards outside the census, are not counted, so tree shade is underestimated. A crown is modelled as a cylinder (radius = half the crown diameter) and lets of the sun through ( in the score; Konarska et al. 2014, 1.3–5.3%). Details and code: github.com/N-iv-a/transit-heat-exposure-dbt.
